        <p>
          Maps from 560 million to 1 billion years ago are generated from the plate reconstructions of Merdith et al. (2021), <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.earscirev.2020.103477">A continuous, kinematic full-plate motion model from 1 Ga to present</a>, Earth-Science Reviews 214, using <a href="https://www.gplates.org/">GPlates</a>.  No elevation data exists for this era, so land, shelves, mountain belts (near subduction zones and where continents collide), ocean depth (from mid-ocean ridges) and coastlines (from an estimated sea level) are approximated from the model, and Snowball Earth glaciations are shown as global ice cover.  These maps are a much rougher approximation than the younger ones.
        </p>
        <p>
          Some elements of this visualization are not adjusted for time (eg. cloud and star positions). The locations are accurate to ~100 km. The coloring of the maps is based on elevation and bathymetry: dark blue = deep water, light blue = shallow water; dark green, green, tan, brown, white = ground in increasing order of elevation.  Before land plants spread (roughly 470 to 400 million years ago), land is shown as bare rock.
